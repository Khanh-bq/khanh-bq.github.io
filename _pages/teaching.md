## Teaching Philosophy

Teaching Computer Science is about more than conveying technical knowledge. I design every course around one question: what is the real problem, and how does this technique help us think about it more clearly? Students need to understand what a tool does, why it was designed, and where it applies — not just how to run it. I call this problem-first thinking, and it is the dividing line between technical literacy and genuine computational thinking.

I use Jupyter notebooks as the primary learning environment — as a structured reasoning space rather than a code editor. Students follow arguments step by step, manipulate data directly, and see how one modeling decision changes the result. Project-based assessments replace examinations where possible, because students who must defend a modeling decision to their peers build a more durable understanding than students who reproduce a formula under time pressure.

## Courses Taught — Hanoi University

### Machine Learning
*3rd-year undergraduate, taught in English*

Covers supervised and unsupervised learning, model selection, regularization, ensemble methods, and neural network fundamentals. Emphasis on understanding the bias-variance tradeoff, interpreting model outputs, and evaluating generalization. Includes lab sessions with scikit-learn and real datasets.

### Artificial Intelligence
*3rd-year undergraduate, taught in English*

Introduces search algorithms, constraint satisfaction, probabilistic reasoning, and introductory topics in natural language processing and computer vision. Students complete a project applying AI methods to a domain of their choosing.

### Data Mining / Big Data Mining
*3rd-year undergraduate, taught in English*

Covers the full data mining pipeline: data preprocessing, association rule mining, classification, clustering, and anomaly detection. Includes practical sessions with Python (pandas, scikit-learn) and exposure to real-world datasets. Recent additions include explainable AI and causal reasoning modules.

### Embedded Systems
*2nd-year undergraduate, taught in English*

Covers combinational and sequential logic, finite-state machines, microcontroller architecture, memory-mapped I/O, interrupts, and sensor interfaces. Lab sessions use hardware prototyping boards alongside simulation tools.

### Python Programming
*1st–2nd year undergraduate*

Introductory programming course covering data types, control flow, functions, file handling, pandas, and basic data visualization. Designed to build computational thinking from the ground up for students with no prior programming experience.

I supervise undergraduate thesis projects in AI and Machine Learning, with a focus on connecting student work to real research problems. Recent supervised projects have contributed to published research in causal discovery and recommender systems.

Students interested in thesis supervision in the areas of explainable AI, learning analytics, or recommender systems are welcome to contact me directly.
